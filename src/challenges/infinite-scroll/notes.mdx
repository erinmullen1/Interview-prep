### The problem

Load a long list in pages, fetching the next page automatically as the user
scrolls near the bottom, instead of a "Page 1 2 3" pagination UI or a manual
"Load More" button.

### The approach

A tiny invisible **sentinel** `<div>` sits at the bottom of the rendered list.
An `IntersectionObserver` watches that sentinel; when it scrolls into the
viewport (or within `rootMargin` of it), the next page is requested.

### Why this over alternatives

- **Scroll event + manual math** (`scrollTop + clientHeight >= scrollHeight`)
  works but requires throttling the handler yourself and is easy to get
  slightly wrong (off-by-a-few-pixels bugs).
- **A "Load More" button** is simpler and more accessible by default (a real
  action for keyboard/screen-reader users to trigger), but wasn't what was
  asked for here. In production, many teams combine both: infinite scroll
  visually, with a manual button as an accessible fallback/escape hatch.

### Trade-offs

- Infinite scroll can trap keyboard and screen-reader users in an
  ever-growing page with no way to reach the footer — a real accessibility
  concern worth raising in an interview even if you implement the scroll
  version.
- It also makes items harder to link/bookmark by position, and can hurt
  perceived performance if pages are large. This demo caps at 120 items.

### What interviewers probe for

- Do you guard against firing multiple simultaneous requests (the `loading`
  check before calling `loadNext`)?
- Do you clean up the observer on unmount / when there's no more data?
- Can you explain **why** you chose `IntersectionObserver` over scroll
  listeners, including its performance characteristics?
