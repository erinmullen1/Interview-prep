### The problem

Build a cart where quantities can change, a bulk discount applies per
product, and shipping depends on the order total — a common "state
management with business rules" exercise.

### The approach

The cart state is kept as small and "dumb" as possible: `{ [productId]:
quantity }`. Nothing else — not subtotal, not discount, not total — is
stored in state. All of it is **derived** inside one `useMemo`, in a single
pass over the cart entries.

This matters because a cart bug you see constantly in real take-homes is
storing the total as its own `useState` and updating it in multiple places
(`addItem`, `removeItem`, `applyDiscount`...) — it's easy for those updates
to drift out of sync. Deriving everything from one source of truth makes
that class of bug impossible.

### The business rules, made explicit

- **Bulk discount**: 10% off a line when that line's quantity reaches 3,
  computed per-product-line (not cart-wide).
- **Free shipping**: applies when the post-discount total is $400+;
  otherwise a flat $15 shipping fee.

These are the kind of rules a real spec often leaves ambiguous (pre- or
post-discount threshold? per-line or per-cart discount stacking?) — flagging
the ambiguity out loud is itself a signal interviewers look for.

### Trade-offs

- **Recomputing everything on every change** vs. incremental updates:
  recomputing (as done here) is simpler and impossible to get out of sync;
  for a cart with thousands of line items you might reach for more granular
  memoization, but that's premature for a cart with a handful of products.
- **`Record<string, number>`** vs. an array of `{productId, qty}` objects:
  the record makes "does this product exist in the cart" and "increment this
  product's quantity" O(1) lookups/updates, at the cost of needing
  `Object.entries` to iterate for display.

### What interviewers probe for

- Is there ever a path where the displayed total can be wrong or stale?
- Do quantity controls handle the qty-reaches-0 case (removing the line
  entirely, not leaving a "0" row)?
- Can you clearly explain the business rules in your own words, including
  their edge cases?
